Winter solstice

We want to celebrate with you the beautiful paradox of the winter solstice: when the longest, darkest night of the year arrives, the light also begins its return.

Rituals are part of life. They help us share as a group, recognise one another and accompany each other through the changes and cycles we go through.

The winter solstice invites us to pause for a moment, to look inward and to remember that life also transforms in stillness. That not all growth needs to be visible and that, even in the longest night, the light slowly begins its return.

This 20 December we want to come together to celebrate that moment of transition: a gathering to care for ourselves, explore the body, and share movement, contact and creativity.

Activities

The warmth of winter (15:30 – 16:00)

Welcome tea · Mujeres Medicina card · Introduction to the solstice

Giving importance to arriving and landing: sharing a warm cup of tea, drawing a card and opening up the meaning of this day. A small ritual to prepare ourselves for the arrival of winter and for that moment when the light begins its return.

Registration.

Simultaneous workshops (16:30 – 18:30)

Two movement proposals and one drawing proposal.

Contact improvisation · Fusion Dance · Gestalt Creative Movement: Warmth and Refuge, with Manu Paredes and Sayil Johansson

Exploring two ways of approaching movement, contact and relationship.

Manu and Sayil invite us to a joint class where two disciplines enter into dialogue: Contact Impro and Fusion Dance, enriched by their backgrounds in somatics and gestalt.

A universe in which to explore and discover warmth as a way of listening to movement and as a place of encounter. And the embrace, in its different forms, as a space for creativity, support, presence, connection and play.

Two perspectives, two dance languages that meet and enrich each other. A space to explore how we approach one another, how we support each other, how we receive and how we offer; how we can move from curiosity, care and the freedom of each body.

An invitation to experience the warmth of contact and the possibility of finding, in the embrace and in shared dance, a small refuge in the middle of winter.

Registration.

Drawing movement: Rhythm, flow & line, with Sus Lledó

Movement drawing workshop with Sus Lledó · Fine Arts graduate

What if we could draw movement instead of trying to depict only bodies?

In this workshop we will approach dance through observation and drawing. We will watch, live, the movement happening in the dance space, paying attention to its rhythms, trajectories, gestures, encounters and ways of relating.

Guided by Sus Lledó, a Fine Arts graduate, we will explore different ways of transferring what we see and perceive onto paper: we will learn to capture the essence of a movement through lines, strokes, shapes and compositions, letting the rhythm and flow of the dance inspire our hand.

We are not trying to reproduce bodies faithfully or make perfect drawings, but to train a sensitive eye, experiment with the stroke and discover how movement can become drawing.

An invitation to observe in a different way, let ourselves be carried by the rhythm and find on paper a new way of dancing.

The water within us: Shiatsu massage in pairs with Irene Gonzalez (20:00 – 21:30)

Winter meridians · Kidney and Bladder

In winter, nature invites us to draw inward, conserve our energy and listen to what needs care.

In the tradition of Chinese medicine, winter is associated with the Water Element and the Kidney and Bladder meridians, symbolically linked to rest, vitality and our capacity to support ourselves.

We will begin with an introduction to traditional Chinese medicine, the Water Element and the pathways of these meridians. Then we will move on to practical exploration in pairs, approaching them through shiatsu-inspired techniques, pressure points, stretches and gentle adjustments, using support and traction to discover different ways of mobilising the body and accompanying another person's movement.

No previous experience is needed, just curiosity and a desire to explore.
